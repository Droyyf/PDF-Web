# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Researchers and academics assembling reference material. They pull citation pages out of source
PDFs, pair each with a cover sheet, and produce clean, often framed, exportable documents for
theses, papers, submissions, reading packets, and personal archives.

Their context is focused, detail-heavy work: the page numbers must be right, the selected pages must
be the correct ones, and exporting many documents at once must be predictable. Sessions can run long
and involve reading dense pages, so legibility and low eye-strain matter.

## Product Purpose

PDF Composer composes PDF pages entirely in the browser, at any size. The user loads one or more
PDFs, picks a cover page and citation pages, arranges them (side by side, or cover overlaid on top),
optionally wraps them in a decorative frame (built-in or user-uploaded), previews every selected page
live, and exports: single file, per document, or per page, as PDF or image.

Success is a researcher turning a pile of sources into correctly-referenced, good-looking composed
pages in a couple of minutes, with the output faithful to what they saw on screen.

## Brand Personality

Warm brutalism. The voice is precise, plain-spoken, and confident, with no decorative filler. Three
words: raw, exact, warm.

The product should feel like an honest instrument: structure shown rather than hidden, weight and
typography doing the work, and the craft expressed through the document and its frames rather than
through gloss. Brutalism supplies the backbone (exposed grid, heavy type, stark contrast); paper-and
ink warmth and the ornate frames keep it from feeling cold or industrial. This honors the app's
original brutalist intent, warmed for long scholarly use.

## Anti-references

- Trendy dark-neon: neon-on-black, glassmorphism, crypto / AI-startup gradients. Explicitly rejected.
- Soft generic SaaS: rounded gray cards in identical grids, a single blue accent, hero-metric panels.
  Brutalism rejects this polish by definition.
- Literal skeuomorphism: faux leather textures, stitching, glossy buttons. Craft should be felt
  through structure and type, not imitated as surface.

## Design Principles

1. Structure made visible. Expose the grid, the seams, and the hierarchy; favor honesty over
   decoration. The interface should read as built, not styled.
2. The document is the hero. The UI is a workbench: pages and frames get the spotlight, and the chrome
   stays raw, plain, and quiet around them.
3. Precision you can trust. Exact page references, unambiguous state, no guessing. A research tool is
   correct first; the design must make correctness legible at a glance.
4. Warmth through material, not gloss. Warmth comes from paper-and-ink tones, weight, and the craft of
   the frames. Never from gradients, glass, or glow.
5. Confident and plain-spoken. Type and copy carry the design. Say things directly; every word and
   every rule earns its place.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG AA: at least 4.5:1 contrast for body text, always-visible focus states, full keyboard
  operability.
- High-contrast for long reading and aging eyes: large, legible type and low glare. The warm cream
  background with near-black ink is chosen partly for sustained-reading comfort.
- Never rely on color alone for state. Citation and cover selection must also read through icon,
  shape, or label, not just hue.
- Honor prefers-reduced-motion: the cover drag/resize and any transitions must degrade gracefully,
  and no essential information is conveyed by motion alone.
